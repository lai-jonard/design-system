la-step-marker

The marker on a step, in either register of the status standard (decision 104). variant="sequence" is a numbered circle for ordered steps: primary while it’s the step in hand, green once it’s done. variant="check" is a check-circle for unordered sub-tasks inside one step. One place for the mapping, so a page can’t drift from the standard. It shows a status; it isn’t pressed.

Anatomy

  1. Mark. The 22px circle (sequence, --la-step-marker-size) or the 20px icon (check, --la-step-marker-check-size). part="mark".
  2. Glyph. The step’s number while it’s pending or active; a check once complete; a lock, a warning, a spinner or a bent arrow for the other statuses.
  3. Name. Not drawn: the marker is named for assistive tech, “Step 2: In progress”, in the paired badge’s words. label="" makes it decorative, for a row that says the state in text.

The marker on a step, in either register of the status standard (decision 104). The sequence variant is a numbered circle for ordered steps: primary while it’s the step in hand, green once it’s done. The check variant is a check-circle for unordered sub-tasks inside one step. One place for the mapping, so a page can’t drift from the standard. It shows a status; it isn’t pressed.

Usage examples

A step in a run. The row says the state in words, so the marker is decorative.

<la-step-marker index="3" status="active" label=""></la-step-marker>

A sub-task inside one step.

<la-step-marker variant="check" status="complete"></la-step-marker>

A step moved past with a reason (decision 115).

<la-step-marker index="2" status="passed"></la-step-marker>

Notes

  • Two registers (decision 104, status-conventions.md). Sequence for ordered steps: the number says where you are, a check says done. Check for unordered sub-tasks inside one step.
  • Green means done, in both registers (decision 104, amended 9 Oct 2026). Active keeps the primary colour, so the step in hand and a finished one differ in colour and in glyph. Beyond that, colour is for the exceptions: attention takes the danger surface and a warning.
  • Processing waits for after launch. It was Auto mode’s, which isn’t in Phase B.
  • Passed (decision 115). A step moved past with a reason: no fill, a 1.5px dashed ring in --la-color-text-faint and a bent arrow in --la-color-text-muted, so it reads neither as done nor as still to do. A step that doesn’t apply isn’t a status.
  • Words. The accessible name uses the paired badge’s words, which are GOV.UK’s task statuses: Completed, In progress, Not yet started, Cannot start yet, There is a problem. Processing and Moved past are ours; GOV.UK has neither.
  • Not pressable. A step is finished from its own button, not by ticking the marker. The marker had a pressable mode until 9 Oct 2026; nothing used it.
  • Numbers. Two digits fit the circle; 100 and above overflow it. A playbook run has far fewer steps.
  • Motion. The number and the check cross-fade (fade, scale, a touch of blur); instant under prefers-reduced-motion.
  • Consumers. la-playbook-step: its step’s marker (sequence) and its parts row (check). In new-design, the Orion playbook run and the e-sign ceremony.
